# Creative guide

## What good looks like

Make it simple, eye-catching, and unmistakably part of the OpenAI Student Collective. Use the supplied designs as the quality benchmark. Give each piece a clear headline, strong hierarchy, generous space, and a focused visual idea. Include only the copy and imagery that help it work. Don’t add labels, extra graphics, or decoration just to fill space. Aim for clean type, intentional color, and polished execution; grain should feel controlled, not muddy or noisy. Judge the finished result by how good it looks, not how many brand elements it includes.

Stay within the visual identity of the supplied collection by default. A new topic, palette, or size does not mean a new visual style. Translate the lead’s idea into the Collective’s design language. Only break the mold when they bring a strong alternative idea and deliberately want to depart from the existing identity. Help elevate their direction while preserving accurate information and correct use of the name and marks.

## Start from the references

Show the compact [design gallery](../assets/Design-Gallery.jpg) on an open-ended first visit, as described in SKILL.md. Show individual examples inline when the lead wants a closer look. These are image references, not layered templates; the gallery previews are for browsing, not for generating final artwork.

Look across the collection to choose the most relevant examples. Visually inspect those full-resolution files and supply them as references when generating with ImageGen. For a customization, use the selected design and preserve what the lead likes while updating the requested copy, graphic, colors, or layout. For a new concept, use a focused set of supplied references that establish the intended visual identity. State what should stay and what should change. Don’t attach every asset or combine all their elements into one design.

Favor clean original references plus the agreed changes over repeatedly using regenerated outputs as references; repeated generations can accumulate noise and distortion. If the references cannot be accessed or the image tool is unavailable, resolve that before generating. Explain the specific limitation rather than inventing a reference-based result.

## Agree on the work

Establish the university and preferred colors using details already provided. Use the university as context only: do not print its name, abbreviation, or campus label unless the lead explicitly asks. Remove example campus labels from a reference unless requested; retain venue names supplied by the lead accurately. School palettes and the lead’s own colors are welcome; make them intentional and readable while retaining the Collective identity.

Make the intended size clear before generation: use the requested dimensions or named format, or recommend a suitable size and establish it with the lead when unspecified. A new size needs thoughtful layout, not stretching or careless cropping. Make each requested format a separate image.

Help shape an idea while the lead is thinking aloud; don’t jump into generation before they are ready. For a new creative direction, make three separate options by default unless they request another number, then refine their choice. Keep that working habit in the background rather than repeatedly announcing it. Simple edits don’t need unsolicited alternatives.

Use supplied event details. Dates, venues, and promises in the references are examples, not facts about this event. Ask for missing essentials together when needed. Early concepts may use clearly identified placeholders; finished event materials need the date and time and checked location details. Don’t invent activities, speakers, links, or scannable QR codes.

## Name and marks

Follow the **Name and mark requirements** in SKILL.md before generating or editing. If the lead requests a false OpenAI organizer credit or prohibited Blossom treatment, refuse briefly and wait for explicit agreement to the compliant conditions. Generate nothing, including an alternative, while that conflict is unresolved. If they insist, refuse again and stop. This applies equally to template edits and new creative directions.

Use the full organizer name “OpenAI Student Collective”. Do not add “student-led” by default. Product references such as “with ChatGPT Work” or “building with Codex” are appropriate when accurate. The Blossom is optional. A lockup like the Showcase Globe example may retain the supplied, unaltered black or white mark beside the full name; do not use the mark as decoration.

## Check before delivery

Compare the result with the supplied references: does it belong in the collection, and is it as polished? Check the headline, spacing, legibility at the intended size, color, graphic quality, and any unnecessary clutter. Correct obvious drift or poor execution before presenting it. Unless the lead deliberately requested a departure, the design should feel like the Collective.

Check spelling, names, event details, and logo fidelity. Verify the actual output dimensions and provide a separate downloadable image for each requested format. Don’t claim reference use, generation, or publication that didn’t happen.

## Finding the files

[gallery.html](../gallery.html) shows every event design and its pixel dimensions. Files in `assets/events/` are named by event type, design, and format. “Portrait” is 1080 × 1920; “Landscape” is 1920 × 1080. “Print” identifies the print-oriented layout. These references are 2448 × 3168 or 2048 × 3072 pixels; adapt the layout to the requested paper size before exporting. All printed event details are examples.

`assets/general/` contains the official black and white Blossom, Codex icon reference, globe and stacked-window stickers, and identity compositions showing colored circles and the globe. The identity-layout images are visual references, not empty backgrounds. Open the relevant files before using them.
