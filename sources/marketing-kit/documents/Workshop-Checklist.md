# Workshop checklist

Use this as background for workshop preparation and broader advice when relevant. It is not an intake flow or a prerequisite for making graphics. Read it at skill startup, but don’t recite it or make the lead complete it unless they ask for help planning a workshop.

Use this checklist for every workshop you host on campus. Work through it with your co-Lead, using one shared deck and event.

## 1. Make a copy of the workshop master deck

Use the master deck for every workshop you host. Make a copy and follow its structure, including the built-in check-in and check-out slides. You’ll add your chosen activity and event details as you plan.

- Share your copy with your co-Lead so you can work from the same deck.

[Make a copy of the master deck](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1FU1VX_P4qxmOz9oQevVLDsRq5an4F7PFUbYi4I1E7sw/copy)

## 2. Choose your audience and activity

### Choose who you’ll bring together

- Co-host with an existing club, class, or campus community. This can be a good starting point: they already have people to invite and may be able to help with the event.
- Or gather students yourself through your campus networks.

If a potential co-host is connected to an AI company, please check with the program team first.

### Choose the activity

Think about who you’re gathering and what they’d find useful. Choose an activity that fits their interests and experience with AI.

Our 10 activities are beginner-friendly, ready to run, and can all be completed on a phone or computer using ChatGPT Work. They’re easy to remix, so use what you know about your campus to make the workshop valuable for the people coming—or create your own activity.

### Give your workshop a name

You’ll want to choose a title that makes your event appealing and easy to understand. Focus on what students will do or make. “Create your own portfolio” or “Build your club’s website” gives someone a concrete reason to come—without needing to know the tools already.

## 3. Create the event in your university’s Luma calendar

### Set the date, time, and place

Pick a date and time, then book a room with a screen and space for students to work on laptops. Coordinate with any campus organization or hosts you’re partnering with, and check your campus’s booking process and event requirements. If you hit a roadblock, reach out to the program team.

Don’t have a Luma calendar for your university? Reach out to the program team.

The Hub’s calendar guide covers calendar setup.

Once you know when and where you can host, make a new event in your university’s Luma calendar:

Keep registration open so interested students can join.

[Create a Luma event](https://luma.com/create)

## 4. Submit it to the central OpenAI Student Collective calendar

Open the central calendar, select Add Event → Add Existing Luma Event, and paste your event link.

The program team will review it to make sure everything looks good.

[Open the central calendar](https://luma.com/openai-studentcollective)

## 5. Promote your event on campus

We encourage you to invite people from the communities and circles you’re already part of, then get creative with how you promote your event. We have some guidelines for promoting your event, so please read the marketing guide before getting started.

See [Marketing guide](Marketing-Guide.md).

We’ve also created a Marketing Materials skill for ChatGPT Work to help you make materials for your event. Download it below and upload it to a new chat to get started.

## 6. During your workshop

Arrive early with your co-Lead to test the screen and decide who’ll lead the slides and who’ll help students as they work.

### Welcome students and help them check in

Ask students to scan the QR code or open the check-in link in your slides. The check-in system looks for an approved workshop at your university on the central Collective Luma calendar, then guides students through claiming ChatGPT Plus and accessing ChatGPT Work.

Give everyone a few minutes to complete the steps before starting. If someone can’t get access, help them join a teammate and flag the issue to the program team.

### Run the activity

Use your deck to introduce the activity, then give students time to work. Walk around, help anyone who’s stuck, and invite students to show each other what they’ve tried. Take a few photos along the way. Aim for students to spend most of the workshop working and building on each other’s ideas.

### Close together

Use the closing slides for check-out and invite students to join your campus community. Share your community channel so they can hear about upcoming events and stay connected.

## 7. Celebrate by sharing a recap in Slack

We’d love to hear how your event went! Share a recap with the entire Campus Lead Collective in Slack. This also serves as your event report—once you’ve submitted it, you’re all done.

[Share your recap in Slack](https://openai.enterprise.slack.com/archives/C0BRS7QULGK)
