Workshops give students hands-on experience with ChatGPT through a guided activity. Help everyone get started, then have students work in small groups to develop their ideas and compare what they make.

Choose an activity that fits your audience and adapt the examples to their interests. A website workshop could help a club organize its resources; a personal-assistant workshop could help students manage their projects. Make the workshop your own—adapt the activity, format, and pace to suit your group’s interests and experience level.

As students work, circulate and help them improve what they’re making. Finish by inviting a few students to share. Students should leave having built something themselves, with a clearer sense of how ChatGPT can support their own interests and work.

Choose a title that makes your event appealing and easy to understand. Focus on what students will make, learn, or try. “Create your own portfolio” or “Practice for your next interview” gives someone a concrete reason to come, even if they haven’t used the tools before.

Create a Luma event
Use the details from your plan above to set up your event on Luma. Make sure it includes the following so your event can be approved:

Title: Name the workshop topic so students know what they’ll make, learn, or try—for example, “Build your personal website”. You can add “x [Club]” if you’re co-hosting.
Description: Explain the activity students will do and what they’ll leave with. If you mention OpenAI in the title or description, use “OpenAI Student Collective”. Don’t present the event as hosted directly by OpenAI.
Event image: Add an image that fits the workshop.
When and where: Add the date, accurate start and end times, and the location.
Registration: Keep RSVPs open to anyone.

Welcome students and help them check in
Check-in opens 2 hours before your event’s start time on Luma, as long as your event is approved on the Global Luma calendar. Ask students to scan the QR code or open the check-in link in your slides to check in and claim ChatGPT Plus.

Give everyone a few minutes to complete the steps before starting. If someone can’t get access, help them partner up for the event and flag the issue to the program team.

Run the activity
Use your deck to introduce the activity, then give students time to work. Walk around, help anyone who’s stuck, and invite students to show each other what they’ve tried. Take a few photos along the way. Aim for students to spend most of the workshop working and building on each other’s ideas.

Check students out
Ask students to complete the check-out form in your closing slides before they leave. Give everyone a few minutes to share their feedback.

Students can check out up to 3 hours after the event’s end time on Luma.
Invite students to your community and Studio Sessions
